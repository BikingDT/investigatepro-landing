pSIF Spotter.

Train your eye to see what could have killed. The difference between a near miss and a fatality is often luck; this app builds the skill to recognise potential serious injury and fatality events before the luck runs out.

Join the list Why pSIF matters

Why recognising pSIF matters.

Serious events are usually preceded by near misses. The question is whether anyone recognised them.

The iceberg

Behind every fatality sit many near misses with the same potential. Most go unrecognised and unreported.

Outcome bias

We judge an event by what happened, not by what could have happened. A minor injury gets minor attention, even when it was nearly fatal.

A skill you can train

Recognising pSIF is not intuition. It improves with practice, and practice is what the app gives.

The pSIF assessment.

The same five questions in every scenario.

  1. Energy

    What hazardous energies were present?

  2. Exposure

    How exposed was the person?

  3. Variability

    What made this outcome low in severity?

  4. Counterfactual

    Change one variable: does the severity change?

  5. Classify

    pSIF: yes, no, or escalate.

Try two scenarios.

From the planned library. Decide, then read the assessment.

Mining

A haul truck reversed close to a light vehicle. The driver of the light vehicle saw it coming and moved forward. No contact was made.

Could this have resulted in serious injury or death?

pSIF: yes. The energy difference between a haul truck and a light vehicle is extreme. The only thing that prevented a fatality was the driver’s awareness; with a blind spot, fatigue or distraction, this would have been fatal.
Construction

A worker stepped on a nail that went through their work boot. First aid only; they were back at work the next day.

Could this have resulted in serious injury or death?

pSIF: no. Painful, but the energy involved has limited severity potential. Even in worse circumstances, serious injury or death is unlikely. It is a housekeeping issue.
