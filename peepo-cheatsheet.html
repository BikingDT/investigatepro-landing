What the card covers.

The same content as the PDF, on the page.

P

People

The person and the team.

  • Physical state: Fatigue and sleep, illness or injury, impairment, physical capability
  • Mental state: Stress and pressure, distraction, complacency, rushing
  • Knowledge: Training, experience, hazard awareness
E

Environment

Where the work happened.

  • Physical: Weather, lighting, noise, temperature, ventilation
  • Workspace: Housekeeping, layout and access, ground conditions, congestion
  • Time: Time of day, shift patterns, remote location
E

Equipment

What the work was done with.

  • Design: Suitability for the task, ergonomics, human factors, fail-safe design
  • Condition: Maintenance status, wear, known defects, pre-use checks
  • Safety features: Guards, interlocks, emergency stops, adequate PPE
P

Procedures

How the work was meant to be done.

  • Availability: The procedure exists, is accessible and is in a usable format
  • Quality: Accurate, complete, clear and up to date
  • Use: Known, trained, followed, practical and enforced
O

Organisation

The decisions behind all of the above.

  • Systems: Risk assessment, change management, permits, contractor management
  • Resources: Staffing, budget, time and schedules
  • Culture: Safety priority, reporting, leadership commitment, competing pressures

Four questions to keep asking.

They take the analysis from the event to the system.

  • When you find human errorAsk why the error was possible, and what system allowed it. Do not stop at blame.
  • When a procedure was not followedAsk whether it was practical, whether there was time, and whether there was pressure to shortcut it.
  • For every P, E, E and P factorAsk what organisational decision created the condition.
  • Before you close the investigationAsk whether fixing this will actually prevent it happening again.
